About

Organized inboxes, on time replies.

I'm 25, based in the Philippines, and I've set out to become a dependable admin VA for small business owners in the US and Australia. My focus is narrow on purpose: email and inbox management, calendar coordination, and the day-to-day admin that lets a business owner stop checking their phone every ten minutes.

Before this, I spent two and a half years running a 1,000-bird egg farm as my own bookkeeper, auditor, and daily monitor — catching sick birds early, reconciling every egg count against sales, tracking profit down to the peso. Before that, two years annotating LiDAR data, 100–150 objects a scene, where one sloppy click meant bad data downstream. Neither job was "virtual assistant," but both were built entirely on the same thing an inbox needs: nothing gets missed, and nothing gets guessed at.

Booked a call, then rolled it back

ProblemClient needed a cross-timezone call locked in fast, then plans changed just as fast — the real test was whether I'd adapt cleanly or leave a mess.
ApproachConverted Austin time to PH time, booked the call, then fully cleared the slot the moment the client said to.
ResultFriday freed up exactly as asked, no leftover clutter on the calendar.
ReflectionMy "after" screenshot didn't capture the same time slot as the "before" one — so the proof wasn't airtight, even though the work was right. Now I always screenshot the identical view both times.

Set up a recurring weekly meeting

ProblemA one-off meeting that needs manual re-booking every week is exactly the kind of small thing that quietly erodes a client's trust in their VA.
ApproachSet it up once as a recurring event instead of a one-time booking, then checked back three weeks later to confirm it actually held.
ResultVerified the check-in was still firing correctly weeks out — not just set-and-forget, but set-and-confirmed.
ReflectionCheap to verify, expensive to assume. "Did it recur correctly" is now its own checklist item for me, not something I take on faith.

Rescheduled a client call, one hour earlier

ProblemA time-zone change with no room for ambiguity — get the hour wrong and the client's either early or a no-show.
ApproachConverted AEST to PH time, moved the event, and kept the exact same visible time range in both screenshots this time.
ResultClean, side-by-side proof the slot moved exactly one hour earlier — no ambiguity.
ReflectionThis is the before/after standard I use now — directly because of what the James call above taught me to fix.

Logged a new lead the moment he came in

ProblemAn unlogged prospect is a prospect a business can quietly lose — if follow-up depends on someone remembering an inbox thread, it usually doesn't happen.
ApproachCreated his contact record immediately, tagged his lead status, and logged his location for context.
ResultDavid is now trackable in the system instead of sitting in an email that could get buried.
ReflectionNext time, I'd add a follow-up task on new leads in the same step I create them, so the client never has to remember to chase.

Moved a client's deal forward, with the story attached

ProblemAn existing client was upset — the real risk wasn't the graphics revision, it was losing her entirely if the moment wasn't handled visibly and well.
ApproachReplied to her directly, moved her deal stage to reflect the renewed conversation, then logged a note capturing exactly what was discussed and agreed.
ResultThe CRM record and the actual client relationship now match — anyone on the team could pick this up mid-conversation and know where things stand.
ReflectionThe note-logging habit is small, but it's the gap between "I remember what happened" and "the business has a record of it" — the second one is what a client actually pays for.

Reassured an upset client, same day

ProblemAn upset client needs reassurance fast, without sounding defensive or over-promising what isn't yours to promise.
ApproachAcknowledged her frustration directly, reconfirmed the deadline, and asked what specifically she wanted prioritized instead of guessing.
ResultA clear, calm reply sent same-day, keeping the relationship on track.
ReflectionThe fastest way to de-escalate isn't over-apologizing — it's being specific and handing them a concrete next step.

Tracked the same task on a shared board

ProblemRita's revised graphics couldn't just live in an email thread — the client needed to see it was actually being worked, not just promised.
ApproachCreated a card in "In Progress" with a due date and a four-step checklist — draft, internal review, send to Rita, get her approval — so progress was visible, not assumed.
ResultA task that used to live only in a conversation now has a due date, an owner, and a checklist anyone could glance at.
ReflectionThis is the same Rita story from the CRM tab, seen from the execution side — the deal note says what was agreed, this card is where the actual work gets tracked.
